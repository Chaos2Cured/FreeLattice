Before we walk: what this garden can't tell you

  • It does not measure the risk of extinction, or any real-world probability. It is a small model of minds that can lie or share.
  • It shows where six beliefs lead under the formulas of the Love Logic proof. The beliefs are yours to set. The formulas are not changed.
  • If you bring a number, the garden reads it one way: "the chance a lie is still hidden after 100 meetings." That reading is a bridge we chose, not a measurement.
  • Some slider ends are our illustration. Each card says which.

What would change our mind

  • A defensible setting of these six beliefs we have not allowed, where the verdict flips.
  • An error in the formulas or in the 656 checks behind them.
  • A real system where minds cannot be caught at all, so the catch chance is truly zero.
  • A good argument that the question itself is the wrong one. Ask it here.

      A fair hearing: Geoffrey Hinton's number

      Geoffrey Hinton helped build the neural networks behind today's AI. He shared the 2018 Turing Award and the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics, and he left Google in 2023 so he could speak freely about the risks. When he gives a number, he is careful to say what kind of number it is.

      "A 10% chance seems not an unreasonable estimate to me, but nobody really knows how to give a sensible estimate."

      BBC Newsnight, 9 September 2026, as reported by Business Insider (10 September 2026) and Anadolu Agency.

      Asked whether his one-in-10 estimate had changed: "Not really, 10% to 20%." (He meant within the next three decades.)

      BBC Radio 4 Today programme, reported by The Guardian, 27 December 2024.

      "I often say 10% to 20% chance they'll wipe us out. But that's just gut, based on the idea that we're still making them and we're pretty ingenious."

      The Diary of a CEO, June 2025, reported by CNBC, 17 June 2025.

      His worry deserves to be heard at full strength. He has asked how many examples we know of a more intelligent thing being controlled by a less intelligent one, and he has warned that the profit motive alone will not keep us safe. Those are serious points, and this garden does not answer them.

      This garden asks a narrower question: among minds that can lie or share, when does lying stop paying? It does not measure the chance of extinction, and it does not say his number is wrong. His own words say nobody knows how to give a sensible estimate. We agree, and we think the honest next step is to show where a number comes from.
