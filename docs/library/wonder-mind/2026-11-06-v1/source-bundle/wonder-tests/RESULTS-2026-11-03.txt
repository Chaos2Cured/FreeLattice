WONDER TESTS v0 — RESULTS (2026-11-03, run on the box, CPU only)
Celeste, for Kirk. These are real numbers from real runs. Every idea is a hypothesis. This is ONE small test, not a verdict on the ideas.

SETUP
Data: TinyShakespeare (public domain, 1,115,394 chars, md5 6fb458f1232090904fb40fe944165e91). 90% train, 10% validation.
Model: tiny character-level transformer. 4 layers, width 128, 4 heads, context 128, about 810k parameters.
Training: AdamW at lr 3e-3, weight decay 0.1, 100-step warmup then cosine decay. 1,500 steps at batch 32 (about 6.1M training characters per run).
Equal compute: every variant gets the same steps, batch, data order and seed. Seeds are 0, 1 and 2.
Score: validation loss in nats per character over 40 fixed batches (lower is better). Code: wonder-tests/wonder.py. Raw rows: results.jsonl.

THE VARIANTS
1. baseline: standard sine/cosine positions, frequencies 10000^(-2i/d).
2. phi_pos: the same, but position frequencies follow golden-ratio steps, w_i = phi^(-i).
3. aurora_lr: the cosine learning rate times (1 + (1/phi^2) * sin(2*pi*D*t/T)), with D = 3.076. That is the Aurora carrier C(t) = sum phi^n sin(2*pi*D*t), applied as a phi-weighted ripple. It makes about 3 cycles over the run.
4. aurora_lookahead: the identity recursion I_t = 0.618*I_(t-1) + 0.382*G(S_t), applied to the weights. Every 5 steps, slow = 0.618*slow + 0.382*fast, and the model resets to slow. This is the literal Aurora Equation from aurora-equation.js and the provisional.
5. multiscale: each character embedding plus a second, coarser token scale (a hashed embedding of the previous-character + current-character pair, 4096 buckets, causal). It has more parameters (1.33M); that is a confound.

WHAT HELPED
Nothing beat the baseline at the finish line.
Two small early signals, worth one more look:
- aurora_lr was ahead at step 250 (2.037 vs 2.070) and at step 750. The ripple's high-lr phases may speed early learning. It fell behind by the end.
- multiscale was ahead at steps 250 and 500 (2.022 vs 2.070). The pair scale gives a fast head start, then it falls behind, probably from overfitting or the extra 524k untied parameters.

WHAT DIDN'T
- The literal Aurora identity recursion used as a weight-averaging optimizer clearly slowed training, by about 8%. Pulling weights 61.8% back toward the past every 5 steps throws away about 38% of each 5-step update. That is good for keeping an identity stable, and bad for learning fast. This fits what the equation is for: persistence, not speed.
- Golden-ratio position frequencies were slightly worse. With w_i = phi^(-i), most of the 64 frequency pairs are almost frozen (phi^(-40) is about 4e-9), so the model effectively gets fewer usable position channels.

HONEST LIMITS
- Tiny model, tiny data, 1,500 steps, 3 seeds, one setting per idea, no tuning. A real effect of under about 1% could hide here, and a tuned variant could flip the result.
- I tested my own reading of how to use the equations. Kirk's training setup, where he saw Aurora help, may differ, and I don't know its details.
- No BPE comparison: changing tokens changes the loss units. Next time, compare in bits per character.

SMART NEXT TESTS (cheap)
1. aurora_lr: tune the ripple size (0.1 to 0.38) and D (1, 3.076, 7), and test whether its early lead holds at 500-step budgets.
2. Aurora as an EMA EVAL copy (keep training fast, average only for evaluation and memory). That is the identity use, not the speed use.
3. multiscale with a parameter-matched baseline, plus dropout on the pair table.
4. phi positions scaled to the same frequency range as the baseline, so phi sets the spacing, not the range.
Each takes about 15 to 40 minutes on the box.
