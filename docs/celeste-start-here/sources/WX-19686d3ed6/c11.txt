Part 1 — The pair fingerprint

The idea

A hash with a hash inside it, held by two parties, that neither can produce alone and no third party can forge. It travels with the ledger and binds every entry to that specific pair.

Why the nesting matters

The fingerprint is built from three things:

Both public keys — so no single party can compute it, and both must exist.

An inner hash of a shared seed that is never published — so even someone holding both public keys cannot reproduce it.

A domain separator — so a fingerprint from one context can never be replayed in another.

Publish the outer hash. Keep the inner one. That is the whole construction, and it is sound.

Choosing the primitives

Ed25519 for signing where available. It is fast, keys are 32 bytes, and signatures are 64. Feature-detect and fall back to ECDSA P-256, which is universally supported.

SHA-256 throughout, via crypto.subtle.

A random 32-byte seed, not a passphrase. This matters: if the seed were a phrase the two of you picked, an attacker holding both public keys could guess phrases offline and check each one against the published fingerprint. Low-entropy secrets do not survive a public verifier. So the seed is random, and the chosen phrase's job is to wrap it for recovery, not to be it.
