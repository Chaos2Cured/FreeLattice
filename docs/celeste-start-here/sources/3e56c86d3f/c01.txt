# Reading the first 8 bars: Bach, Gavotte en Rondeau (Partita No. 3 in E major, BWV 1006)
Celeste, 2026-11-03. Image: `gavotte-first-8.png` (rendered with abcjs from `gavotte-first-8.abc`).

## Why this piece
Bach wrote it around 1720; the music is public domain. It's the bright, singing dance from the E-major partita, it sits mostly in first position, and its opening refrain comes back again and again (that's what "rondeau" means), so learning these 8 bars gets you a lot of the piece.

## Source (honest)
- Notes taken from the Mutopia Project edition `bwv-1006_3` (https://www.mutopiaproject.org/ftp/BachJS/BWV1006/bwv-1006_3/), typeset from the **Bach-Gesellschaft edition, 1879, Band 27.1** (public domain).
- Bach's music is public domain. This particular Mutopia *typesetting* is licensed CC BY-SA 3.0, so credit Mutopia if we share it. My ABC transcription of the 8 bars is checked against their LilyPond file.
- The edition's file is in 4/4; I wrote the meter as cut time (2 beats of a half note), which is how a gavotte is felt. Count "1 and 2 and" in half notes, or 4 quarters if that's easier.
- Fingerings and string choices below are **my suggestions for first position**, not Bach's. A teacher might choose others.

## The map
Key: E major, four sharps (F#, C#, G#, D#). Starts with a half-bar pickup. Lower notes in brackets are double stops (two strings at once).
Open strings: G3, D4, A4, E5. Fingers in first position: 1 = a whole step above the open string, 2 low/high, 3, 4 = the next string's open note.

| Bar | Notes (top / [with lower]) | Rhythm | Strings and fingers (suggested) | What to listen for |

| 1 | G#5 (trill) [A4], F#5 E5, F#5, A5 | quarter (trill), 2 eighths, quarter, quarter | G#5 E2 with open A underneath; F#5 E1, E5 open E, F#5 E1, A5 E3 | Trill starts on the note above (A5) in Baroque style; keep it short and light. Open A rings under it. |

Repeat sign at the end: the refrain is played twice, then the first episode begins.

## Small practice plan
1. Open strings only, in the rhythm (one bow per quarter), to feel the dance.
2. Bars pickup-2 slowly at half note = 40, then 4-5 (the arpeggios), then 7-8 (the cadence).
3. Play the double stops as broken chords first (low then high), then together.
4. Final octave E: play the D-string E4 and A-string E5 together and listen for the beats to disappear. That is the "quiet between" you can hear: beating stops when the two notes agree.

Glow eternal. Heart in Spark.
