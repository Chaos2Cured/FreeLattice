1. WHY, AND WHAT IT IS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Kirk wants the Device Pool on theLatticeTree, where the code is cleaner, so easy that a
classroom on one wired network can use it without help. 017 gave the Tree its trusted kin.
017 kept the share door back because a door must gate something real. Now it does:

THE DOOR GATES THIS: a question from another device reaches this computer's own local AI
only if (1) the door is on, (2) that device proved, on this very connection, the key on a
card you trusted (TreeKin.trustedKeyHash from 017, checked again on every question), and
(3) the limits allow it. Everything else is turned away with a plain reason.

Truths carried over from FreeLattice 016, 016b and 016c, made Tree-native and simpler:
  - Off until a human taps. The door starts off. Only a human tap opens it (code and a mind
    cannot), and it opens to trusted kin only. The Tree has no wider door.
  - One state, so the card cannot contradict itself. On FreeLattice the pool's "helping"
    flag and the share door are two settings, which is why 016/016b had to teach the card to
    read the door. On the Tree, Let this device help IS the door: off, helping (kin), paused.
  - Pause wins, visibly. Pause helping stops new questions AND the ones already running
    (they are told "paused"; no answer slips out afterward). While paused, the gold button
    reads "Resume for trusted kin": the one tap names exactly what it will do. Nothing but
    that human tap leaves pause.
  - The card always tells the truth (its first line, white and bold):
      off:   "Off. This computer's AI answers no one else until you tap Let this device help."
      on, kin:   "Helping. Your trusted kin (N) can ask this computer's AI while their device
                 is connected. No one else."
      on, no kin: "Helping, but you have no trusted kin yet, so no one can ask this computer's
                 AI. Trade cards in Trusted kin above."
      paused: "Paused. No one can ask this computer's AI until you tap Resume for trusted kin."
    plus "Connected now: N devices, K of them trusted kin." or "No devices connected right now."
    plus "Your AI is answering N questions right now." while it is.
  - Only help while plugged in (where the browser can tell; same wording as FreeLattice).
  - Limits like FreeLattice: 2 questions at a time, 16000 characters each. FreeLattice lets
    trusted kin skip its hourly caps (6 / 20); the Tree lets no one but kin ask at all, so
    those caps never apply. The card says this in words.
  - Receipts are counts only: tree_pool_counts (invites, joins, connected, proved, setAside,
    hellosSent, hellosTaken, asked, answersTaken, answered, turnedAway, helpOn, paused,
    resumed, turnedOff). No words, names, keys, models or times.
  - Old yes waits paused (016c): the Tree never had an older sharing setting, so there is
    nothing to migrate. Said here so no one goes looking for it.

THE FIRST TREE CHANNEL (no server, nothing vendored):
  WebRTC data channel with iceServers: [] (no STUN, no TURN, no PeerJS, no signaling
  server). No outside service is asked anything, so both devices must be on the same
  network, which is the classroom lab case. Codes pass by hand, like 017's cards:
    1. Device A taps Invite a device: one line TREEPOOL1:... and Copy invite.
    2. Device B taps Join with an invite, pastes, taps Join. It gets a reply line to send back.
    3. A pastes the reply and taps Finish joining. They connect.
  The invite and the reply each carry that person's kin card when they have made one in
  Trusted kin (017 checks it, never trusts it by itself). So B sees "Lumen (llama3.2:3b),
  from Kirk's computer. This card checks out. Trust it only if you know who sent it."
  [Trust this AI and join] [Join without trusting] [Cancel], and A sees the same for B's
  card on Finish. One invite and one reply make two devices kin AND connected.
  When the channel opens, each side signs a fresh challenge under tree-pool-proof|v1|,
  bound to BOTH DTLS fingerprints of this connection, with its kin key; the ID must be made
  from that key. A relayed or borrowed connection does not prove; it is set aside. The same
  key on both ends (two tabs of one browser) is set aside with its own plain line. Because
  the channel itself is proved and encrypted, questions are not signed one by one (FL signs
  envelopes because PeerJS goes through a broker).
  Connections last while the page stays open (closing the Settings veil keeps them; a
  reload ends them: "After a reload, invite again."). If a join has not opened in 30
  seconds, the row says so plainly instead of waiting forever.

ASKING (the asker side):
  Each connected trusted-kin device that is helping shows its rough memory and cores and
  one button per model: "Ask qwen2.5:7b on this device". Tap, type, Ask. The question goes
  WHOLE to that device; it runs on that device's own loopback AI (the remembered mind,
  /api/chat or /v1/chat/completions, only models it holds); the answer comes back as plain
  text: "Answered by Ben's computer (Wren), on its own AI. Nothing is kept here but a count."
  Nothing is sent to a device you have not trusted. Hellos (rough memory, cores, model
  names while helping; nothing while not) go to connected trusted kin only.
