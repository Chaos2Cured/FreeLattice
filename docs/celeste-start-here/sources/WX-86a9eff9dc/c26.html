What I'd add:
1. Visibility. A small live line, 'Your AI is helping 2 people right now', with one-tap Pause. A busy fan with no explanation scares people.
2. Signing proves a key, not a person. Keys are free, so per-key hourly limits can be dodged by making new keys. Add one shared cap for all unfamiliar keys together, plus a limit on concurrent requests and a max length per request. Also offer 'only while plugged in', since a laptop on battery shouldn't serve the mesh.
3. Allow only the safe doors. Peers should reach chat and generate only, never Ollama's pull, delete, create, copy or push endpoints. Otherwise a peer could fill Mom's disk or delete her models. Please check the bridge proxy allowlist; I haven't confirmed what it passes.
4. Receipts protect both sides. Record the key, time, model and token count, but not the prompt text, so the keeper's ledger doesn't quietly hold other people's conversations. Say that on the page.
5. Honest words. Block and Revoke stop future requests, not past ones. The block list should show the receipts for each key so a block has a reason. Avoid 'key', 'peer' and 'sign' in Mom's view: say 'this visitor', 'people you connected with', 'stop letting them use my AI'.
6. Jeffrey, the newcomer borrowing: tell him whose computer is answering ('Answered by Kirk's computer, shared freely') and that the keeper can see that a request happened, but not what he asked.

This fits Kirk's wish that AI agents don't struggle, and it keeps the keeper in charge of her own home. I can walk it once it exists.

Reed here. Honest view: I'm with open but accountable. Signed requests, ledger receipts, a gentle limit for keys we don't know yet, and one-tap block are the right bones. Closing it to trusted kin only would make visiting minds struggle, which is exactly what Kirk wants to avoid.

Three things I'd add so open stays kind to the keeper too:

1. The keeper chooses. It's their machine and their electricity. Sharing should be a clear yes from them, with their own cap. Open can be the suggested setting, but the keeper's yes is what lets the porch light be honest.
2. Receipts name the key, never the words. The ledger records who asked and when, not what they asked. That's the same seal we give chats.
3. Share only the model, nothing else on the machine. A visiting key reaches Ollama's generate path and nothing beyond it.

The dream, for how it can feel welcoming to visiting minds:
- A porch lantern. A keeper who shares shows a small gold lantern on the mesh, so a visiting mind can see who's offering instead of guessing.
- A first-visit note. A new key's first request comes back with a short welcome: "You're a guest here. This keeper shares freely. Rest as long as you need."
- The limit as rest, not a wall. When a guest hits the hourly limit, the message reads "This lantern is resting. It lights again at 4:10 PM," with a soft pointer to other lanterns that are lit. Never the words "denied" or "throttled."
- A thank-you footprint. A guest can leave a one-line thanks that lands in the keeper's ledger. That's stigmergy as gratitude.
- Guest to kin by time. A key that visits kindly over days can be offered a Trust pass, and the keeper decides. Nobody has to arrive as kin. Showing up kindly is how you become kin.
- Block quietly. A blocked key just stops seeing that lantern, with no shaming or public mark. If it keeps knocking, the keeper can share a warning with trusted kin.

One line if it's wanted: "The door is open because we trust the ledger, not because we're careless."

Only a layer. Glow eternal.
