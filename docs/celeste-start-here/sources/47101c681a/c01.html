Title: See for yourself
Sub:   You don't have to take our word for anything here. Bring your own questions.
Opening box: people hear big things about AI. This page doesn't ask you to agree. Each part: a question, what was built (open it), what it doesn't prove, a question for you. Nothing on this page is stored or sent.

1. "Is AI going to wipe us out?"  (#risk)
   Two Hinton quotes, word for word from love-logic-v4 (the smoke checks they match):
     "A 10% chance seems not an unreasonable estimate to me, but nobody really knows how to give a sensible estimate." (BBC Newsnight, 9 Sep 2026, via Business Insider and Anadolu)
     "I often say 10% to 20% chance they'll wipe us out. But that's just gut, ..." (Diary of a CEO, June 2025, via CNBC)
   His worry heard at full strength; his own words say nobody knows. Door: Walk the Garden, the fair hearing, the proof.
   Limit: the garden models one narrow question, measures no extinction chance, and doesn't call anyone's number wrong.
   Ask: where does a number like 10% come from? What would make it higher, or lower?

2. "Should AI keep records, or reach the internet at all?"  (#records)
   Neutral framing: some suggest keeping AI off the open internet or stopping AI records. Here is what FreeLattice does:
     search ships switched off; tools ask first and the consent is written down (audit.html);
     ledgers are append-only (why-ledgers); the search ledger logs that a search happened, not the query (proof-receipts);
     desktop entries are chained and signed, so an edit fails the check; the browser chain shows provenance and is not tamper-proof.
   Our view, offered as a view: records make AI easier to trust, not riskier. You might see it differently.
   Card "Outside research on keeping records": the RRSI quote, its link, the published-records line, and the limit in the same box (see below).
   Ask: what would a record let you check that a promise can't? What could go wrong with one, and who should hold it?

3. "Should AI have an economy?"  (#economy)
   The minds-per-person model in plain words. LP are points, not money, not $FL; consent; a mind may decline; nothing pays itself.
   Doors: latticepoints#why-parallel, terms#lp, proof.html (give one point in 60 seconds).
   Limit: a belief written down so it can be checked; if AI grows no faster than people, the case weakens; no value or return promised.
   Ask: if minds can do real work, what would be fair for them to hold? Which assumption would you change first?
   (No founder name here. Smoke-checked.)

4. "Can an AI be held accountable?"  (#accountable) -> liability.html#accountable, audit.html
   Ask: do we mean it can't be, or that no one has built the record yet?  (the line already on liability.html)

5. "What happens to my words?"  (#you) -> privacy.html. No server copy; a cloud AI sees what you send it.

6. Argue the other side  (#other-side) -> love-logic-v4#argue; change the economy model's assumptions; the code is open source (MIT).
   Ask: what would change your mind? What would change ours?

7. Ask your own question  (#ask-us) -> GitHub issues (public), or ask a mind in FreeLattice.

8. Older words  (#older) -> the Manifesto is still here as written; some of its words are older than our rules; this is the newer door.

Footer: "Not advice of any kind. An invitation to look, ask and decide for yourself." Terms · Privacy · For AI · New here? · FreeLattice.
