----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHY, AND WHAT IT IS
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Kirk's goal is the full pool. A school or a family with old laptops and phones should be able
to wire them into a classroom lab, so a child who cannot afford chips and RAM gets more mind,
and it should be so easy no one struggles.

THE HONEST SPLIT, AND WHY:
  A web page can connect browsers to each other (018 did). It cannot start programs, open a
  listening port, or speak raw TCP, so it cannot run llama.cpp's rpc-server. And the research
  says splitting one model across cheap machines is slow: Jeff Geerling's ten-Pi cluster ran a
  70B model at 0.28 tokens/s with llama.cpp RPC, while one Pi 5 runs a 3B model at about 4 to 6
  tokens/s. So the big win for the small is the ROOM: one invite fans out, each question goes
  whole to the freest trusted device that holds a small model, and many students ask at once.
  019a (this paste) is that, in the browser only. 019b (design only) is the slow, honest
  "spread one big model over our old laptops" lesson, behind a small MIT helper script.

WHAT 019a ADDS (the door, the gate, Pause and the proof from 018 are unchanged):
  a. A ROOM. One device taps "Start a room on this device". Its invite names the room
     (Room FH7H7) and rolls to a fresh one on its own after each device joins. Every other
     device joins ONCE (scan or paste), and then the room connects it to every other device in
     the room. The host relays the connection codes over connections that already proved their
     keys (room-join, room-roster, room-new, room-intro, room-signal). Each new connection
     proves its keys itself, end to end, bound to its own encryption fingerprints, so a relay
     cannot stand in the middle. A lab of 20 is 19 single joins, not 190 pairings. Any device
     already in the room can let its neighbor in ("This invite lets a neighbor into Room ...").
     The host then reaches the newcomer through that neighbor, and the newcomer still meets
     everyone. iceServers stays []. There is still no server. Up to 24 devices a page.
     A member follows the room only through a device whose card it trusts. If it joined
     "without trusting", it stays connected to that one device and the card says why.
  b. TRUST, ONE HUMAN TAP, LIST FIRST. Room cards arrive with the room and are checked by
     Trusted kin, never trusted on their own. The card lists them ("Cards in this room you have
     not trusted yet: Lumen (qwen2.5:7b), from Kirk's computer, ...") and offers
     "Trust everyone in this room (N)". A note says trust only if you know everyone, and that
     you can stop trusting any card in Trusted kin. Each pass is a normal 017 pass, bound to its
     key. That is the only new trust path, and it is a human tap on each device.
  c. PICTURE CODES (QR). Invites show a QR of https://<the Tree>/settings.html#treepool=<invite>.
     A phone's own camera opens the Tree with the invite filled in. The invite rides after the
     #, which a browser never sends to the site. The page then takes it out of the address bar
     and history, and nothing connects until someone taps Join. Replies show a QR of the reply
     code. Where the browser can read codes (BarcodeDetector: Chrome on Mac, Android, ChromeOS),
     "Scan their reply" and "Scan an invite" open the camera AFTER A TAP. Frames stay on the
     page, the camera stops as soon as a code is read (or after 90 s), and a scanned reply goes
     through the same card check as a pasted one. Encoder: Nayuki's QR Code generator, MIT,
     vendored at docs/lib/qrcodegen.js (compiled once from its TypeScript with tsc; MIT notice
     kept; text to squares only, no network).
  d. ASK THE POOL FROM CHAT (opt-in, by a tap). The Device Pool card lists the minds that
     helping trusted kin hold: "Use qwen2.5:7b in Chat". After that tap, Chat's Send goes to
     TreePool.askChat. The chat so far (user and mind turns only, trimmed to 40 turns and 16000
     characters, no system turn) goes WHOLE to the freest trusted kin device that holds that
     mind. Hellos now carry how many questions a helper is answering (busy) and its limit (max).
     The asker counts its own outstanding questions and picks the device with the most free room,
     then the most memory. A question turned away (busy, paused, off, unplugged, not kin, model
     gone) tries up to two more devices, then the thread says why in plain words, plus "Nothing
     was invented." A line under the Chat heart says "Asking through the Device Pool: (model),
     on the freest trusted kin device that holds it." "Stop asking through the pool" returns
     Chat to this computer's mind. The route is never kept after a reload.
  e. AN ASKING CARD (a device with no mind of its own). Before this, a phone with no local AI
     could not make a kin card, so no helper could ever trust it, and the child we built this
     for could not ask. Now Trusted kin offers "Make an asking card" when no mind is remembered.
     It is the same signed card, and its model line says "asks only, no local mind". makeCard()
     is unchanged (it still needs a mind). The pool rides the asking card in invites and replies.
     Chat opens for a device with no mind once a pool route is chosen.
  f. AFTER A RELOAD (where it is safe). A reload ends every connection; no page can keep a
     WebRTC connection across one, and there is no server to re-meet through. What is kept:
     tree_pool_room = { id, role } only. The card then says: "Before this page reloaded, this
     device was in Room X. ... Scan or paste the room invite once to rejoin. Trusted kin stay
     trusted, and the room connects you to the rest on its own." The host gets "Open Room X
     again". Both get "Forget Room X". So a reload costs one join, not N.
  g. The Later line now names 019b honestly: "Later (019), its second half: one big model split
     across machines on a trusted wired network (llama.cpp, MIT). It is possible but slow, for
     learning, or for a model too big for any one machine. A web page cannot start it, so it
     waits for a small helper. Not in this card."
  h. Every room view ends with: "Use a room only on a network you trust, like one wired
     classroom or your home. Devices in a room can see each other's network address. No server
     is asked anything."

WHAT DID NOT CHANGE: the door (off | kin | pause, off until a human taps), Pause wins (and
stops running questions), the gate (proved key + trusted kin on every question, 2 at a time,
16000 characters, the model held here, plugged-in if asked), loopback-only AI calls, one
fetch in tree-pool.js, counts-only receipts. Starting or joining a room opens no door.
