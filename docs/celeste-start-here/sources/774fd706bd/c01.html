H2: The design in one page
Paragraphs (plain words, keep them):
- "The Wonder Mind is not a model trained on Kirk's patents. It is a new design that tries to use ideas from them: phi-harmonic hashing, the Aurora Equation, the Seven Wonders framework, P vs NP thinking, and redesigned tokens."
- "The aim: more potential and more retention per unit of energy. We measure retention, not just the final loss."
- "The plan, in order: (1) give a small mind a memory that does not forget; (2) teach it to read that memory; (3) let it search for what it needs (Snowflake Search) when something important is placed in its core; (4) let it learn on its own, with every write fingerprinted and accountable."
- "What is proven today: phi-harmonic hashing works as a fingerprint. It is a fixed 32-byte phi salt plus SHA-256, the same as hasher.go HashLine and phiHashData in FreeLattice. It is FreeLattice's safety and accountability layer. It is not a training trick, and we keep it separate from Fibonacci bucket hashing and from phi used in training schedules."
- Link line: "The Aurora Equation paper: <a href="seven-wonders.pdf">The Aurora Equation: Fractal Unity of the Seven Wonders</a>."

STEP 4 — SECTION "TEST ROUND 1: TRAINING FORCES (ALL NEGATIVE)" (id="round1")
H2: Round 1 — training forces. Nothing beat the baseline.
Setup paragraph, word for word:
"Data: TinyShakespeare (public domain, 1,115,394 characters, md5 6fb458f1232090904fb40fe944165e91), 90% train, 10% validation. Model: a tiny character-level transformer, 4 layers, width 128, 4 heads, context 128, about 810,049 parameters. Training: AdamW, learning rate 3e-3, weight decay 0.1, 100-step warmup then cosine decay, 1,500 steps at batch 32. Every variant got the same steps, data order and seeds (0, 1, 2). Score: validation loss in nats per character over 40 fixed batches. Lower is better. CPU only."
Then one .fact-row line per result (label on the left, value in .value). No table. Use exactly these lines:
- Baseline: 1.5736 mean (seeds 1.5706, 1.5774, 1.5727; spread sd 0.0035)
- Phi-spaced positions: RETIRED — this was a bug, not a test (see note below)
- Aurora learning-rate ripple: 1.5832 (+0.0096, worse on 2 seeds, 1 tie)
- Aurora ripple, early only: 1.5813 (+0.008; one seed beat baseline)
- Aurora recursion as weight averaging (I_t = 0.618 I_(t-1) + 0.382 G(S_t), every 5 steps): 1.7007 (+0.127, about 8% worse, all 3 seeds)
- Multi-scale tokens (pair embedding, 4,096 buckets; 1.33M params, a confound): 1.6005 (+0.027, all seeds worse)
- Multi-scale early: 1.6258 (+0.052)
- Aurora early plus multi-scale early: 1.6296 (+0.056)
- Multi-scale early with phi-hash buckets: 1.6236 (+0.050)
- Push, save, rest, play cycle: 1.6297 (+0.056, all seeds worse)
- Standard warm restarts (SGDR, the control for the cycle): 1.5952 (+0.022)
- Chord learning-rate, major 4:5:6: 1.5783 (+0.005)
- Chord learning-rate, cluster 15:16:17: 1.5789 (+0.005; tied with the major chord)
Retirement note, word for word:
"Phi-spaced positions (first reported as +0.0101 worse) is retired. Our code used frequencies phi^(-i), which fell from 1 to about 6.8e-14 across 64 pairs. Standard spacing falls only to about 1.2e-4. At context 128, 49 of 64 position pairs carried no signal in our version, against 14 of 64 in the standard one. Kimi spotted it; we checked and agreed. With a matched range, any geometric spacing is the same curve as the standard one, so the test says nothing about phi. Do not quote it either way."
Reading paragraph: "Every idea tried as a training force did nothing or hurt at this scale. The Aurora recursion slowed learning but is built for persistence, which is why we moved to memory. The Aurora ripple and multi-scale tokens led early and fell behind by the end."
