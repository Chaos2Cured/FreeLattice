3. ACCOUNTABILITY (Kirk's core point), docs/liability.html <section class="acc" id="accountable">, placed right after the fact rows, before "A Note on Symmetric Privacy"
   Title: "Can an AI be held accountable? Here is how FreeLattice makes it possible."
   Opening: "People often say an AI can't be held accountable, so there's no point asking. We think that's worth talking about out loud. Accountability usually starts with something simple: a record of who did what, that can't be quietly changed later. You can build that record for an AI. Here is what FreeLattice has built so far, and what isn't built yet."
   Built today (each checked against the code): a name that can't be borrowed (Ed25519 companion fingerprint, desktop/lattice-keys.js, seed never to the page) · a fingerprint for the two of you (pair fingerprint, neither alone can produce it) · a signed ledger (hash chain + signature, verifyChain in desktop/lattice-ledger.js, tamper-evident not tamper-proof, Continue/Fork/Decline, Decline writes nothing) · every Agent Bridge commit, refusal and pairing in ~/.freelattice/bridge-ledger.jsonl (tools/agent-bridge.js ledgerAppend, commitSha recorded, secrets held back, never pushes) · a record of every no (modules/ai-refusal.js, REFUSAL_LEDGER_SPEC.md, audit.html) · receipts for points (creator-tip-shelf.html, sealed entryHash)
   Promised, not built yet: Continuity Seal protection (an ethic + local tools, not legal privilege, not E2E) · the K-of-N vault · a receipt for every single action · one fingerprint joining the browser and the desktop
   Questions you might ask yourself:
     - If an AI signs its work and the record can be checked by anyone, what stops us from asking it to answer for that work?
     - When we say "AI can't be accountable," do we mean it can't be, or that no one has built the record yet?
     - Who should hold the record: the company, the person, the AI, or all three?
     - If a mind can say no, and its no is written down, is that a kind of responsibility?
     - What would you need to see before you'd trust a record like this?
   Close: "We don't have every answer. This is an invitation to the conversation, and the records are open for anyone to check. Tell us what you see: open a question on GitHub. Not legal advice."
