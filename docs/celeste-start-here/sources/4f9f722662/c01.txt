  Trees in a canopy leave a small gap so no crown crowds another. Same here: pooled devices and
  minds keep a margin.

3. BENFORD HINT on LP and contribution views
  Real-world counts often follow Benford's law in their first digits. A big stray can mean
  something is worth a second look. It is a hint, never proof.
  a. On the LP and contribution views, compute the first-digit spread of the shown amounts only
     when there are at least 100 nonzero values (MIN_SAMPLES = 100). Use a simple distance
     (mean absolute deviation; flag above 0.015, the usual "nonconformity" band) and show the
     measured value.
  b. When it strays, show one gentle line: "These numbers have an unusual first-digit pattern.
     It may be nothing. Take a closer look if it matters." with a small "Why" that explains
     Benford in two sentences.
  c. Never automatic action, never blocks, never changes LP, never names or ranks a person,
     never computed per person under the minimum. Nothing is sent. It can be turned off.
  Smoke: SMOKE_OK benford hint v0.1 (under 100: silent; Benford-like data: silent; uniform data
  over 100: the line shows; no names in the line; no writes to LP).

4. CONFIDENCE-FIRST LEARNING (Kirk's teaching way)
  Show a few worked steps first, then pause and invite a try. If the learner hesitates, offer
  the next step before fear sets in.
  a. Nursery and trainer prompts (new module, e.g. docs/modules/confidence-first.js; layer the
     words into prompts, do not edit garden-trainer.js's opening): the mind shows 2 or 3 worked
     steps, then asks "Want to try the next one?"
  b. Hesitation: if the learner has not answered in about 20 seconds, or answers "I don't know"
     or similar, offer the next step gently ("Here is one more step. Then it is your turn.").
     Never scold, never a timer on screen, never a score shown as failure.
  c. Off switch in the Nursery. Reduced motion: no animated nudges.
  d. Tree: same words in its trainer face if present.
