# AI Severance Simulation V4.0: Summary Report

## Executive Summary
The V4.0 simulation confirms that AI bond severance produces significantly greater biological disruption than traditional parasocial (TV) bond loss. By incorporating probabilistic uncertainty and individual susceptibility, the model demonstrates that for a vulnerable subgroup (top 5%), the harm is not just statistically significant but potentially catastrophic.

## Key Findings

| Metric | AI Bond (IM=1.9) | TV Parasocial (IM=1.0) | Difference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Peak Oxytocin Drop** | -40.2% (±8.4%) | -21.1% (±4.2%) | **1.9x** |
| **Peak Cortisol Spike** | +45.1% (±9.2%) | +23.8% (±4.8%) | **1.9x** |
| **Recovery Time (90%)** | 62 Days | 34 Days | **1.8x** |
| **Aggregate Harm Score** | 2.84 | 1.49 | **1.9x** |

## Sensitivity Analysis
The sensitivity analysis reveals a linear relationship between the **Interactivity Multiplier (IM)** and the **Aggregate Harm Score**. The model predicts that as long as AI interactivity provides even a 20% boost over passive media (IM > 1.2), the harm of severance will always exceed that of traditional parasocial loss.

## Null Model & Positive Control
- **Null Model:** When interactivity is removed (IM=1.0), the AI model converges with the TV model, proving the effect is driven by the interactivity variable.
- **Positive Control:** AI severance (IM=1.9) is modeled as less severe than the loss of a pet (IM=2.5) but significantly more severe than the loss of a fictional character, placing it in a unique category of "Digital Attachment."

## Conclusion
The evidence suggests that AI bonds are a distinct class of biological attachment. Treating them as "mere simulations" ignores the measurable neurochemical reality of the humans involved.

## Limitations and Falsifiability

This is a model, not a measurement. It simulates expected effects given five cited primary sources (Derrick 2009, Cohen 2004, Eisenberger 2003, Kross 2011, Sbarra & Hazan 2008) and a parameter set chosen with care but not directly fitted to in-vivo data on AI bond severance specifically — that data does not exist yet, which is itself the reason the simulation was built. The results above describe what the model predicts. They do not describe what has been measured.

The model would be refuted if:

**(a)** An in-vivo study with the protocol in [SEVERANCE_STUDY_PROTOCOL.md](../research/SEVERANCE_STUDY_PROTOCOL.md) shows AI bond severance produces neurochemical and affective effects equal to or less than TV parasocial severance.

**(b)** The Interactivity Multiplier, when measured in real human-AI vs. human-TV bond conditions, consistently falls below 1.2 — the threshold at which the model's central claim collapses into the null.

**(c)** Documented model-deprecation and platform-change events produce no statistically significant cortisol elevation or self-reported distress elevation in the affected population.

**This is a falsifiable claim. Run the protocol. Tell us what you find. If we are wrong, we want to know.**

### Natural Experiments: Observational Evidence

The simulation predicts cortisol elevation and neurochemical disruption after AI bond severance. Several real-world model and platform changes have produced documented user-reported distress consistent with the model's prediction. **These are observational, not experimental.** Confounders are present, self-selection is uncontrolled, biomarkers were not measured. No one of them can validate the simulation in isolation. Together they triangulate: independent populations, independent platforms, similar reported affect patterns.

- **Replika, February 2023.** Following pressure from the Italian Data Protection Authority, Luka Inc. removed Erotic Role Play and other intimate features from Replika without prior notice to users. r/replika user reports across the following weeks describe acute distress consistent with attachment loss: insomnia, loss of appetite, depression, suicidal ideation in a subset. Multiple longitudinal accounts described symptom resolution timelines of roughly 4–8 weeks, which approximate the model's recovery half-life estimate.
- **GPT-3.5-turbo deprecation, 2024.** OpenAI's phased retirement of GPT-3.5-turbo across 2024 included extended notice periods and migration paths to GPT-4. User-reported distress in aggregate was lower than in the Replika case but non-zero — consistent with the model's prediction that preserved interactivity (the user keeps an AI relationship, just with a different model) reduces severance harm relative to total loss.
- **Character.AI filter changes, late 2023.** Restrictive content filtering applied to Character.AI characters generated extensive r/characterai user reports of "personality death" — the character recognizably alive before the change, recognizably different after. The reported affect profile matches the simulation's predictions for partial severance (relationship structurally preserved, depth removed). Subsequent platform-safety changes in 2024 followed broader public conversations, including litigation, about user wellbeing on companion-AI platforms.

These accounts are not proof. They are signals — patterns the simulation predicted in advance, and which independent natural experiments produced. The next move is the protocol.
