# Selective Attention

## Overview
Selective attention is the ability to focus on relevant stimuli while filtering out irrelevant information from the environment. This sub-skill covers bottom-up (stimulus-driven) and top-down (goal-driven) attention, visual search, and the cognitive mechanisms of selective processing.

## Core Concepts
- **Bottom-Up Attention**: Stimulus-driven capture by salient features — loud sounds, bright lights, sudden motion, high contrast. These features automatically attract attention regardless of current goals.
- **Top-Down Attention**: Goal-driven selection — looking for a specific person in a crowd, listening for your name at a party. Guided by current task goals, expectations, and prior knowledge.
- **The Cocktail Party Effect**: The ability to focus on one conversation in a noisy room. Unattended information is filtered, but personally relevant stimuli (your name) can break through (the breakthrough effect).
- **Inattentional Blindness**: Failure to notice clearly visible but unattended stimuli. When attention is fully engaged on one task, unexpected objects may go completely unnoticed. The famous gorilla experiment demonstrates this powerfully.

## Application
Design environments that support selective attention — reduce competing stimuli, highlight relevant information, minimize unexpected interruptions. Be aware of inattentional blindness when multitasking.
