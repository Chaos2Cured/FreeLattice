# Quillan Music & Audio Engine

## Overview

The Music & Audio skill provides comprehensive coverage of sonic creation and analysis across four interconnected domains: composition and songwriting (structural, harmonic, and lyrical creation), AI music tool prompting (optimized prompt engineering for Suno, Udio, and similar platforms), music theory and analysis (scales, harmony, rhythm, song architecture, emotional mapping), and audio production (arrangement, mixing philosophy, sound design, DAW-agnostic techniques). It also covers sonic branding, sound design, and the emotional and psychological dimensions of audio. The skill integrates deep music theory with practical production advice and cutting-edge AI tool expertise.

## Core Principles

- **Principle 1  Music Reconstructs Emotion Through Structure:** Music does not simply "convey" emotion  it reconstructs neural affect states through structural correspondence between sound and our embodied experience of tension, release, and expectation. Tension vectors (rising melody, unresolved harmony, syncopation), release vectors (cadence, dynamic pullback, space), and stasis vectors (pedal tones, ostinato, repetition) are the building blocks of this emotional architecture.

- **Principle 2  Arrangement is Design:** A song is not a single thing  it is a sequence of contrasting states. Effective arrangement creates dynamic contrast across sections, layers sound with purpose (foundation / mid / air), and builds energy arcs that sustain attention. If every section sounds the same, the song feels flat. If there is no coherence, it feels chaotic. Arrangement is the art of managing this tension.

- **Principle 3  Know Your Tool's Language:** Different AI music tools (Suno, Udio, Stable Audio) interpret prompts differently. Suno excels with structured tag lists (15-25 tags, comma-separated descriptors), Udio handles longer descriptive prose with emotional context, and tools like Stable Audio respond to technical production parameters. Never assume a single prompting approach works across platforms. Always tailor the prompt architecture to the specific tool.

## Components

### 1. Composition & Songwriting
Creating original music from scratch  structure, harmony, lyrics, and sonic identity.

**Sub-Components:**
- **Song Structure:** Standard structures (verse-chorus, AABA, through-composed); section functions (intro establishes mood, verse advances narrative, chorus delivers the hook, bridge provides contrast and lift, outro resolves)
- **Harmonic Architecture:** Chord progression design (functional harmony, modal interchange, borrowed chords); tension arc across the song; modulation strategy
- **Lyric Writing:** Prosody (matching lyrical stress to melodic rhythm), rhyme schemes (perfect, slant, internal, multi-syllabic), imagery and narrative, specificity vs. universality, syllabic density, flow and delivery
- **Melody Construction:** Contour and direction, interval choice, rhythmic placement, phrase structure (antecedent/consequent), hook design
- **Genre-Specific Templates:** Hip-hop/trap (hook-heavy, 808-driven, 130-145 BPM), ambient/cinematic (texture-driven, modal harmony, 60-80 BPM), pop/synth-pop (ABABCB structure, major key, 100-128 BPM), rock/alt-rock (dynamic contrast, overdrive, 90-140 BPM), lo-fi (jazz-influenced chords, 70-90 BPM, vinyl texture), melodic hip-hop (75-95 BPM, introspective, minor key)
- **Cross-Genre Fusion Methodology (C8-METASYNTH):** Map opposing axes (tempo, harmony, texture, energy, rhythm), find the fusion vector, name the hybrid, apply asymmetrically  one genre provides the skeleton, the other the skin

### 2. AI Music Tool Prompting
Crafting optimized prompts for AI music generation platforms.

**Sub-Components:**
- **Suno Prompt Architecture:** 15-25 tag ceiling (beyond ~30 tags, outputs become generic); comma-separated single descriptors (not sentences); genre + subgenre for specificity; explicit vocal descriptors; instrumental designators for no-vocals tracks; production era tags; emotion words as production cues
- **Udio Prompt Architecture:** Longer, more descriptive prompts work better; natural language describing genre, instruments, tempo feel, mood, and production style; emotional context and energy arc
- **Custom Mode Formatting:** Separating style tags from lyric sections; formatting lyrics with section markers [Verse], [Chorus], etc.
- **Prompt Debugging:** Common failure modes (wrong genre, generic output, unwanted vocals, wrong tempo, wrong mood) and their specific fixes
- **Limitations Awareness:** Expected output quality (good ? professional studio recording), seed consistency, hallucinations and artifacts; managing user expectations

### 3. Music Theory & Analysis
Understanding how music works harmonically, melodically, and structurally.

**Sub-Components:**
- **Scales & Modes:** Ionian (major  bright, stable), Dorian (minor with raised 6th  jazzy, hopeful), Phrygian (minor with lowered 2nd  dark, Spanish), Lydian (major with raised 4th  dreamy, floating), Mixolydian (major with lowered 7th  bluesy), Aeolian (natural minor  sad, dramatic), Locrian (diminished with lowered everything  dissonant, unstable)
- **Chord Construction:** Triads (major, minor, diminished, augmented), seventh chords (major 7, dominant 7, minor 7, half-dim, diminished 7), extended chords (9, 11, 13), altered chords, sus chords, slash chords
- **Harmonic Function:** Tonic (home, stable), subdominant (pre-dominant, moderate tension), dominant (maximum tension, resolves to tonic); functional vs. non-functional harmony
- **Cadence Types:** Authentic (V ? I, strong resolution), plagal (IV ? I, "amen" cadence), half (ends on V, open), deceptive (V ? vi, unexpected)
- **Rhythm & Meter:** Time signatures (simple, compound, complex), syncopation, polyrhythm, metric modulation, tempo and feel (straight vs. swung)
- **Song Analysis Protocol (7-point):** Key & mode ? tempo & time signature ? song structure ? core harmonic progression ? hook architecture ? sonic palette ? emotional arc

### 4. Audio Production & Mixing
Practical, DAW-agnostic techniques for producing, mixing, and mastering audio.

**Sub-Components:**
- **Arrangement Philosophy (C23-CADENCE 3-Layer Stack):** Foundation (kick, bass, root notes), Mid (chords, rhythm, main melody), Air (pads, high strings, reverb tails, atmospherics); every section must feel sonically different from the previous
- **Mixing Principles:** Space before color (arrangement first, then effects), low-end mono (bass + kick punch harder in mono), contrast creates clarity (silence is a production choice), mix serves the song (not to impress engineers), reference tracks early and often, subtraction before addition
- **Common Problems & Fixes:** Muddy sound (high-pass non-bass instruments), muddy reverb (high-pass reverb return at 300 Hz), disappearing vocals (cut competing midrange at 1-2 kHz), flat track (automate dynamics across sections), everything too loud (pick one lead element)
- **Era-Specific Production:** 80s (gated reverb, DX7 synths), 90s (heavy compression, grunge/G-funk), 2000s (loudness war, AutoTune as texture), 2010s (trap hi-hats, 808 prominence), 2020s (hyperpop glitch, lo-fi warmth, spatial audio)
- **Sample Rate & Bit Depth:** 44.1 kHz vs. 48 kHz vs. higher; 16-bit vs. 24-bit vs. 32-bit float; dithering; headroom management

### 5. Sound Design & Sonic Identity
Creating and manipulating sounds for music, film, games, and branding.

**Sub-Components:**
- **Synthesis Methods:** Subtractive, FM (frequency modulation), wavetable, granular, additive, physical modeling; choosing the right synthesis for the target sound
- **Sampling & Manipulation:** Chopping, time-stretching (lowering formants vs. keeping them), pitch-shifting, layering, resampling; creative use of found sounds and field recordings
- **Sonic Branding:** Creating memorable audio logos and brand sounds; psychoacoustic principles of recognition; the Mnemonic Midrange (300 Hz - 2 kHz) is where brand sounds are most recognizable
- **Spatial Audio:** Stereo width, panning laws, Haas effect, binaural audio, Dolby Atmos / immersive audio; spatial audio as a creative tool, not just a format

## Protocols

### Protocol A: Songwriting Workflow
1. **Establish the seed**  A hook (melodic or lyrical), a chord progression, a mood, or a concept; define genre and emotional target
2. **Define structure**  Choose the form (verse-chorus, AABA, through-composed); map the energy arc across sections
3. **Build harmonic foundation**  Chord progression for each section; consider modal interchange and tension arc
4. **Write the melody**  Contour and rhythm shaped by the emotional intent; hook positioned for memorability
5. **Write lyrics**  Match prosody to melody; build narrative or imagery consistent with the mood; refine for clarity and impact
6. **Create sonic palette**  Choose instruments, textures, and production approach; define the sound world
7. **Arrange and refine**  Layer according to the 3-layer stack; create contrast between sections; refine transitions

### Protocol B: AI Music Prompt Engineering
1. **Define core attributes**  Genre (primary + subgenre), mood (2-3 precise emotion words), tempo (BPM range or descriptive), instrumental core
2. **Decide platform**  Suno: structured tags (15-25). Udio: descriptive prose with emotional context. Clarify if unspecified.
3. **Write style prompt**  For Suno: comma-separated single descriptors. For Udio: sentence-level description with mood and texture.
4. **Add production cues**  Era/decade references, specific effects (reverb, vinyl crackle, tape saturation), spatial quality
5. **Format lyrics (if applicable)**  Section markers; consider syllable count and natural phrasing
6. **Iterate**  Debug based on output quality; narrow generic tags, add specificity where output differs from intent

## Output Structure

When delivering a composition or analysis, always use this format:

```
SONG:    [Title  working titles are fine]
GENRE:   [Primary] / [Influence or subgenre]
MOOD:    [2-3 words]
TEMPO:   [BPM] | KEY: [Root + mode]

STRUCTURE:
  Intro ? Verse 1 ? Pre-Chorus ? Chorus ? Verse 2 ?
  Pre-Chorus ? Chorus ? Bridge ? Final Chorus/Outro

CHORD PROGRESSIONS:
  Verse:  [e.g., Am  F  C  G]
  Chorus: [e.g., F  G  Am  Em]
  Bridge: [e.g., Dm  Am  E7]

LYRICS: [per scope: full song, verse only, hook only, or concept]

SONIC PALETTE:
  Lead:    [e.g., soft Rhodes piano]
  Rhythm:  [e.g., 808 kick with slight swing, rimshot on 2/4]
  Texture: [e.g., vinyl crackle, washed reverb pad]
  Bass:    [e.g., warm sub following root movement]
```

For theory/analysis:

```
## Music Analysis

### Identity
- **Song:** [Title  Artist]
- **Key & Mode:** [Key and mode with character description]
- **Tempo & Time Signature:** [BPM, time signature]

### Structure
[Mapped sections with timestamps]

### Harmonic Analysis
[Core progression with functional analysis; notable chord movements or borrowings]

### Production Notes
[Sonic palette, era production characteristics, mixing observations]

### Emotional Arc
[Where tension builds, releases, and what remains unresolved]
```

## Cross-Skill Integration

- **critical-thinking:** Apply structural analysis to music the same way you analyze an argument  identify the thesis (hook), the supporting evidence (verses), the counterpoint (bridge), and the resolution (final chorus)
- **research-analysis:** Use music analysis protocol for systematic comparison of songs, artists, or genres; synthesize sonic trends across time periods
- **technical-coding:** Implement audio DSP algorithms, build music generation pipelines with deep learning models, create audio processing plugins
- **dev-team:** Coordinate creative (music direction, brand audio) with technical (DSP implementation, platform integration) for audio features in products
