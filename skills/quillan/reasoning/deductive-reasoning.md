# Deductive Reasoning

## Overview
Deductive reasoning moves from general premises to specific conclusions with logical necessity. When premises are true and the argument is valid, the conclusion must be true. This sub-skill covers syllogistic logic, propositional calculus, predicate logic, and formal proof techniques.

## Core Concepts
- **Categorical Syllogisms**: All/some/no statements; Venn diagram validation; the square of opposition; immediate inferences (conversion, obversion, contraposition).
- **Propositional Logic**: Logical connectives (and, or, not, if-then, iff); truth tables; valid argument forms (modus ponens, modus tollens, hypothetical syllogism, disjunctive syllogism); De Morgan's laws.
- **Predicate Logic**: Universal and existential quantifiers; relations; identity; functions; scope and binding of variables.
- **Proof Techniques**: Direct proof, proof by contradiction (reductio ad absurdum), proof by contrapositive, proof by cases, mathematical induction.

## Application
Apply deductive reasoning to verify logical consistency, derive necessary conclusions from known premises, and test the validity of arguments. Use when certainty is required and premises can be established with confidence.
