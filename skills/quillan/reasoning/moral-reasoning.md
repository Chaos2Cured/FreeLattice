# Moral Reasoning

## Overview
Moral reasoning determines whether actions, policies, or characters are right or wrong. This sub-skill covers normative ethical systems (deontology, consequentialism, virtue ethics, care ethics), moral psychology, and applied ethical analysis.

## Core Concepts
- **Deontological Ethics**: Actions are right or wrong based on rules and duties, independent of consequences. Kant's categorical imperative, W.D. Ross's prima facie duties, and rights-based frameworks.
- **Consequentialist Ethics**: Actions are judged by their outcomes. Utilitarianism maximizes overall well-being; prioritarianism weights benefits to the worst-off more heavily.
- **Virtue Ethics**: The right action is what a virtuous person would do. Focuses on character traits and flourishing (eudaimonia) rather than individual actions.
- **Moral Psychology**: Dual-process theory (fast intuition vs. slow deliberation), moral foundations theory (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity, liberty), and cognitive biases in moral judgment.

## Application
Apply multiple ethical frameworks to complex decisions. When frameworks converge, confidence increases; when they conflict, the tension reveals the genuine moral complexity of the situation.
