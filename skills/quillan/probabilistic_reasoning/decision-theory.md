# Decision Theory

## Overview
Decision theory provides the mathematical framework for making optimal choices under uncertainty. This sub-skill covers expected utility maximization, risk analysis, multi-attribute decision-making, and the value of information.

## Core Concepts
- **Expected Utility Theory**: The rational decision maximizes expected utility — the probability-weighted average of outcomes. Utility encodes preferences over outcomes, including risk aversion, loss aversion, and time preference.
- **Risk Analysis**: Risk = probability × consequence for adverse events. Risk management involves identification, assessment, mitigation, and monitoring. Tolerable risk thresholds depend on context.
- **Multi-Attribute Utility**: When decisions involve multiple conflicting objectives (cost, quality, time), multi-attribute utility theory (MAUT) and multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA) provide systematic trade-off frameworks.
- **Value of Information**: The expected gain from gathering additional information before deciding. Perfect information has an expected value (EVPI); sample information has an expected value of sample information (EVSI).

## Application
Structure decisions by enumerating options, uncertainties, and outcomes. Compute expected utilities and identify the optimal choice. Assess sensitivity to probability and utility inputs. Consider whether gathering more information is worthwhile.
