# Statistical Thinking

## Overview
Statistical thinking is the mental framework for understanding variability, designing data collection, and drawing inferences from data. This sub-skill covers the core principles of statistical reasoning — variation, sampling, estimation, hypothesis testing, and common statistical pitfalls.

## Core Concepts
- **Variation**: All measurements vary. Understanding the sources of variation (systematic vs. random, within-group vs. between-group) is essential for interpretation.
- **Sampling and Estimation**: Samples provide information about populations. Sampling distributions, the central limit theorem, and standard errors quantify the uncertainty in estimates.
- **Hypothesis Testing**: Null and alternative hypotheses; Type I and Type II errors; significance levels, p-values, and power. The limitations of NHST (null hypothesis significance testing) and the value of effect sizes.
- **Common Pitfalls**: Data dredging, multiple comparisons, p-hacking, HARKing (hypothesizing after results are known), publication bias, and the replication crisis. Pre-registration, registered reports, and meta-analysis as solutions.

## Application
Apply statistical thinking before data collection — what is the question, what data is needed, what comparisons will be made? During analysis, focus on effect sizes and uncertainty, not just p-values. Communicate results with appropriate caveats.
