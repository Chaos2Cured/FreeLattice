# Analogy Evaluation

## Overview
Analogy evaluation is the systematic assessment of whether an analogy is valid, useful, and reliable. This sub-skill covers criteria for evaluating analogical strength, identifying disanalogies, and determining when an analogy has been over-extended.

## Core Concepts
- **Evaluation Criteria**: Structural consistency (how well relational structures align), semantic plausibility (consistency with target domain knowledge), predictive power (testable predictions generated), explanatory depth (genuine understanding vs. surface similarity), and boundary detection.
- **Disanalogy Identification**: Where the analogy breaks down — properties of the source that do not map to the target, or outcomes predicted by the analogy that contradict target domain knowledge.
- **Over-Extension Risks**: Applying an analogy beyond its valid range. Common in policy debates, scientific reasoning, and product design where strong surface similarities mask deep structural differences.
- **Confidence Assessment**: Confidence should increase with the depth and breadth of structural alignment, the number of verified predictions, and the absence of significant disanalogies.

## Application
Before acting on an analogical inference, evaluate the analogy systematically. Document both where the analogy holds and where it breaks. Adjust confidence based on evaluation results.
