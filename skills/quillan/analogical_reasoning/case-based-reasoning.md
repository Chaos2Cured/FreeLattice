# Case-Based Reasoning

## Overview
Case-based reasoning (CBR) solves new problems by adapting solutions from similar past cases. This sub-skill covers the CBR cycle — retrieve, reuse, revise, retain — and its relationship to analogical reasoning in both cognitive science and AI systems.

## Core Concepts
- **CBR Cycle**: Retrieve (find similar past cases), Reuse (apply the previous solution to the new problem), Revise (adapt the solution if it fails), Retain (store the new case for future use).
- **Case Representation**: How cases are structured for effective retrieval — features, relational structure, outcomes, and contextual information. Case libraries must be indexed for efficient access.
- **Similarity Assessment**: Surface similarity (shared features), structural similarity (shared relational patterns), and pragmatic similarity (shared goals and outcomes). The appropriate similarity metric depends on the domain.
- **Adaptation Strategies**: Substitution (replace elements of the old solution), transformation (restructure the old solution), and derivational analogy (replay the reasoning process that produced the old solution).

## Application
Build case libraries of solved problems and their solutions. When facing new problems, systematically search for structurally similar cases. Adapt past solutions to fit novel contexts.
