# Cross-Domain Transfer

## Overview
Cross-domain transfer is the practical application of knowledge from one domain to another through analogical reasoning. This sub-skill covers how to identify transferable structures, adapt solutions across domains, and avoid negative transfer (applying analogies that lead to incorrect conclusions).

## Core Concepts
- **Transfer Mechanisms**: Knowledge is transferred via relational correspondences, not surface similarities. The deeper the relational match, the more reliable the transfer.
- **Adaptation Requirements**: Transferred knowledge rarely fits perfectly — it must be adapted to the target domain's unique constraints, affordances, and background knowledge.
- **Near vs. Far Transfer**: Near transfer (similar domains, easier mapping, higher confidence) vs. far transfer (distantly related domains, requires more abstraction, lower initial confidence but higher creative potential).
- **Negative Transfer**: When superficial similarities mask deep structural differences, transferred knowledge can lead to incorrect conclusions. Boundary testing is essential.

## Application
Identify transfer opportunities by looking for structural parallels between solved and unsolved problems. Adapt solutions to fit target domain constraints. Actively test for negative transfer by probing where the analogy breaks down.
