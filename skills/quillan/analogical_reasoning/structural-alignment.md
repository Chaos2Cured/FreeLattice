# Structural Alignment

## Overview
Structural alignment is the process of finding systematic correspondences between the relational structures of two domains. This sub-skill covers the computational and cognitive mechanisms of alignment, including the identification of common relational patterns and the resolution of conflicting mappings.

## Core Concepts
- **Alignment as Constraint Satisfaction**: The mapping problem is solved by finding the globally optimal alignment that satisfies structural consistency constraints — parallel connectivity, one-to-one mapping, and systematicity.
- **Predicate Hierarchies**: Higher-order relational predicates (cause, enable, imply) are more important for alignment than first-order relations (larger-than, part-of) or object attributes (red, heavy).
- **Alignment Depth**: Surface alignment (matching object attributes) vs. deep alignment (matching relational structures). Deep alignment produces more robust analogical inferences.
- **Alignment Breakdown**: When structural alignment fails, the analogy is weak or misleading. Common failure modes include attribute-only matches, inconsistent connectivities, and missing predicate hierarchies.

## Application
When evaluating an analogy, assess the depth of structural alignment. Surface-level matches may be misleading. Prioritize higher-order relational matches over attribute or first-order matches for reliable analogical inference.
