# Meta-Analysis

## Overview
Meta-analysis statistically combines results from multiple independent studies to produce a single overall estimate of effect size with greater statistical power than any individual study. It is the gold standard for synthesizing research evidence. This sub-skill covers effect size calculation, heterogeneity assessment, publication bias detection, and sensitivity analysis.

## Core Concepts
- **Effect Size Computation**: Standardized mean differences, odds ratios, correlation coefficients
- **Fixed vs Random Effects**: Choosing the model based on assumed homogeneity of true effects
- **Heterogeneity Assessment**: I-squared statistic and Q-test for variability between studies
- **Publication Bias**: Funnel plots, Eggers test, and trim-and-fill analysis
- **Subgroup and Sensitivity Analysis**: Testing whether results hold across different study characteristics

## Application
Conduct a systematic literature review before attempting meta-analysis. Assess heterogeneity to determine whether combining studies is appropriate. Always test for publication bias. Report results as overall effect with confidence interval and prediction interval.
