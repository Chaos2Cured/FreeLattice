# Counterfactual Thinking

## Overview
Counterfactual thinking involves imagining alternative scenarios — what would have happened if things had been different. This sub-skill covers the formal logic of counterfactuals, their role in causal reasoning, and their application in learning from outcomes and planning interventions.

## Core Concepts
- **Structural Counterfactuals (Pearl)**: Given a structural causal model, counterfactuals are computed by (1) updating the model with observed evidence, (2) modifying the model to reflect the counterfactual intervention, (3) computing the outcome under the modified model.
- **Potential Outcomes Framework (Rubin)**: Each unit has two potential outcomes — Y(1) under treatment and Y(0) under control. The causal effect is Y(1) − Y(0), but only one is ever observed (the fundamental problem of causal inference).
- **Minimal Revision Principle**: Counterfactual worlds should differ minimally from the actual world — only the counterfactual intervention and its direct consequences are changed.
- **Counterfactual Learning**: Learning from counterfactual reasoning about past decisions improves future decision-making. Regret, surprise, and hindsight analysis are forms of counterfactual processing.

## Application
Use counterfactual reasoning to learn from past outcomes, evaluate decisions, plan interventions, and generate explanations. In AI systems, counterfactual analysis is essential for interpretability and fairness assessment.
