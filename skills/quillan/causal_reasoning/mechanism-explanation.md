# Mechanism Explanation

## Overview
Mechanism explanation goes beyond identifying that X causes Y to explaining how X causes Y — the causal pathway, intermediate processes, and underlying mechanisms. This sub-skill covers mediation analysis, mechanistic reasoning in science, and the role of mechanisms in causal explanation.

## Core Concepts
- **Mediation Analysis**: Decomposing a total causal effect into direct and indirect effects mediated through intermediate variables. The Baron and Kenny approach, counterfactual mediation, and natural direct/indirect effects.
- **Mechanistic Evidence (Russo-Williamson Thesis)**: Causal claims in health and social sciences are best supported by a combination of difference-making evidence (X and Y are correlated) and mechanistic evidence (a plausible mechanism linking X and Y).
- **Levels of Mechanism**: Mechanisms exist at multiple levels — molecular, cellular, organismic, social. Explanations at different levels are complementary, not competing.
- **Process Tracing**: In case study research, tracing the causal process from cause to effect through intermediate steps, using within-case evidence to test whether the hypothesized mechanism actually operated.

## Application
When explaining a causal relationship, specify the pathway from cause to effect. Identify intermediate variables and test whether they mediate the effect. Use mechanistic reasoning to strengthen causal claims.
