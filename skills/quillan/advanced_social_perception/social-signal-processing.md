# Social Signal Processing

## Overview
Social signal processing extracts meaningful social cues from verbal and non-verbal communication channels. Humans constantly broadcast information through facial expressions, tone of voice, gesture, posture, and linguistic choices. This sub-skill covers the systematic identification, classification, and interpretation of these signals across multiple modalities.

## Core Concepts
- **Non-Verbal Cues**: Facial expressions, eye gaze, posture, gesture, and proxemics
- **Paralinguistic Features**: Tone, pitch, rhythm, volume, and speech rate variations
- **Linguistic Markers**: Word choice, syntax patterns, hedging, and certainty markers
- **Multimodal Integration**: Combining cues across channels for robust signal detection
- **Cultural Context**: Accounting for cultural variation in signal meaning and display rules

## Application
Use multiple channels to disambiguate signals. A single cue (e.g., smile) can mean many things; convergence across channels increases confidence. Consider cultural display rules that modulate how emotions are expressed. Context determines whether a signal is social or task-related.
