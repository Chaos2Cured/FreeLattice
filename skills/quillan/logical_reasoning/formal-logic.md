# Formal Logic

## Overview
Formal logic provides the mathematical framework for analyzing the structure of valid arguments independent of their content. This sub-skill covers propositional calculus, predicate logic, formal proof systems, and the metatheoretical properties of logical systems — soundness, completeness, and decidability.

## Core Concepts
- **Propositional Calculus**: Atomic propositions and logical connectives (not, and, or, implies, iff). Truth tables determine validity. Tautologies, contradictions, and contingencies. Normal forms (CNF, DNF).
- **Predicate Logic**: Quantifiers (universal ∀, existential ∃), relations, functions, and identity. Free and bound variables. Order-sorted and many-sorted logics.
- **Natural Deduction**: A formal proof system with introduction and elimination rules for each connective and quantifier. Fitch-style and Gentzen-style presentations.
- **Metatheory**: Soundness (if provable then valid), completeness (if valid then provable), compactness, Löwenheim-Skolem theorems, and the limits of formalization (incompleteness, undecidability).

## Application
Use formal logic to specify and verify systems, analyze argument validity, and detect hidden contradictions. In AI, formal logic underpins knowledge representation, automated theorem proving, and formal verification.
