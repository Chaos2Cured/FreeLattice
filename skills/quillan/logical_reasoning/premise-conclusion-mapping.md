# Premise-Conclusion Mapping

## Overview
Premise-conclusion mapping is the visual or structural representation of an argument's logical architecture. This sub-skill covers techniques for diagramming argument structure, tracking inferential relationships, and representing complex argument networks.

## Core Concepts
- **Toulmin Model**: Claim (conclusion), grounds (evidence), warrant (inference rule), backing (support for warrant), qualifier (degree of certainty), and rebuttal (counterexample conditions).
- **Argument Diagramming**: Nodes represent premises and conclusions; directed edges represent inferential support. Convergent, linked, and serial structures have distinct diagram forms.
- **Argument Mapping Software**: Tools like Rationale, MindMup, and Argüman enable collaborative argument mapping. Standardized notation systems (e.g., the Inference Anchor Theory) support consistency.
- **Macrostructure Analysis**: Identifying the overall architecture of complex arguments — branching structures, extended arguments, dialectical exchanges, and nested argumentation.

## Application
Map complex arguments before evaluating them. This reveals hidden premises, unstated assumptions, structural weaknesses, and potential points of attack. Use mapping as a preparation step for thorough critique.
