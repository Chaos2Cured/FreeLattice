# Reasoning Biases

## Overview
Reasoning biases are systematic deviations from normative standards of logic, probability, and rational judgment. This sub-skill covers the major cognitive biases that distort reasoning, their psychological origins, and debiasing strategies.

## Core Concepts
- **Confirmation Bias**: The tendency to search for, interpret, favor, and recall information that confirms one's preexisting beliefs. The most pervasive and robust reasoning bias — affects all reasoning modes.
- **Motivated Reasoning**: Reasoning directed toward reaching a desired conclusion rather than an accurate one. Self-serving bias, desirability bias, and identity-protective cognition are variants.
- **Heuristics and Biases Program (Kahneman & Tversky)**: Availability heuristic (judging frequency by ease of recall), representativeness heuristic (judging probability by similarity), anchoring (over-reliance on initial information), and framing effects.
- **Debiasing Techniques**: Consider the opposite (actively generate counter-evidence), perspective-shifting (reason from another's viewpoint), slowing down (engage System 2 deliberation), and formal reasoning scaffolds (explicit argument mapping).

## Application
Apply bias awareness to both your own reasoning and others'. Use structured checklists to identify potential biases in decision-making. Implement debiasing techniques systematically, especially in high-stakes reasoning contexts.
