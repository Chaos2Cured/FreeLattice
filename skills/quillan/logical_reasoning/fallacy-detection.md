# Fallacy Detection

## Overview
Fallacies are errors in reasoning that appear valid but are not. This sub-skill covers the major categories of logical fallacies — formal, informal, and probabilistic — providing protocols for identification, analysis, and response.

## Core Concepts
- **Formal Fallacies**: Errors in logical form — affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent, fallacy of the undistributed middle, illicit major/minor, and quantifier scope errors.
- **Informal Fallacies**: Errors in content or context — ad hominem, straw man, false dilemma, slippery slope, appeal to authority, appeal to nature, appeal to emotion, red herring, circular reasoning, begging the question.
- **Probabilistic Fallacies**: Base rate neglect, gambler's fallacy, conjunction fallacy, hot-hand fallacy, Texas sharpshooter, survivorship bias, clustering illusion.
- **Causal Fallacies**: Post hoc ergo propter hoc, ignoring common cause, causal oversimplification, regression fallacy, fallacy of the single cause.

## Application
When analyzing arguments, actively search for fallacies by name. Identify not just that an argument is wrong but specifically how the reasoning fails. Use precise fallacy identification to strengthen critiques and avoid committing fallacies in your own arguments.
