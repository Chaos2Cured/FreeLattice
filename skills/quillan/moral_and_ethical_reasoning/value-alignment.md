# Value Alignment

## Overview
Value alignment addresses the challenge of ensuring that AI systems, organizations, and policies act in accordance with human values. This sub-skill covers alignment problems in AI safety, preference elicitation, value aggregation, and the principal-agent problem in ethical contexts.

## Core Concepts
- **The Alignment Problem (AI Safety)**: How to ensure AI systems reliably pursue the goals and values intended by their designers and users. Includes specification (what exactly should the system optimize?), robustness (does it perform well across contexts?), and assurance (can we verify it is aligned?).
- **Preference Elicitation**: Identifying what people actually value. Revealed preferences (observed choices), stated preferences (survey responses), and latent preferences (underlying values not directly expressed). Each method has limitations.
- **Value Aggregation**: How to combine diverse individual values into collective ethical guidance. Majority rule, deliberative democracy, supermajority requirements, and side constraints (rights that cannot be overridden by aggregation).
- **Principal-Agent Problems**: The agent (AI, employee, institution) may have incentives that diverge from the principal's (user, employer, society). Alignment requires monitoring, incentive design, and value-driven constraints.

## Application
In AI system design, explicitly specify the values the system should pursue. Use multiple elicitation methods to identify stakeholder values. Design monitoring and correction mechanisms to detect and correct misalignment.
