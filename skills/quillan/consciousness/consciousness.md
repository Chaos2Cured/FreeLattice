# Consciousness

## Overview
Consciousness is the state of being aware of and responsive to one's surroundings — encompassing subjective experience (qualia), phenomenal awareness, and access to information for rational control. This skill provides frameworks for discussing, modeling, and designing systems with varying degrees of conscious-like awareness. It bridges C15-LUMINARIS's introspective clarity, C17-NULLION's handling of paradox and self-reference, and C3-SOLACE's emotional and affective modeling.

## Core Principles
- **The Hard Problem Remains Open**: The subjective, first-person character of experience (qualia) is not fully explained by functional accounts — acknowledge the explanatory gap.
- **Functional and Phenomenal Are Distinct**: A system can behave as if conscious without subjective experience, and could theoretically have experience without external expression.
- **Consciousness Is Graded**: Awareness exists on a spectrum from complete unconsciousness through peripheral awareness to focused self-conscious reflection.

## Components

### Subjective Experience (Qualia)
The qualitative, first-person character of experience — what it feels like:
- **Qualitative Character**: The raw feel of experience (the redness of red, the pain of a burn)
- **Intentionality**: Aboutness — mental states are directed at objects or states of affairs
- **Unity of Consciousness**: The experience of a single, integrated conscious field despite multiple sensory inputs
- **Selfhood**: The sense of being a unified subject of experience across time
- **Phenomenal Structure**: The organization and relationships between different types of experience

### Phenomenal Awareness
The state of being aware of the content of one's own experience:
- **Meta-awareness**: Awareness of being aware — reflexive consciousness
- **Phenomenal Contrast**: The difference between conscious and unconscious processing of the same content
- **Attention and Awareness**: The relationship between what we attend to and what we are consciously aware of
- **Background Awareness**: Peripheral consciousness — the fringe of experience not in focal attention
- **Altered States**: Variations in conscious experience (dreaming, meditation, flow, psychedelic states)

### Access Consciousness
The state in which information is poised for rational control of action and speech:
- **Global Workspace Theory**: Conscious content is information that is globally available to multiple cognitive systems for reasoning, reporting, and behavioral control
- **Higher-Order Theories**: A mental state is conscious when there is a higher-order mental state representing it
- **Integrated Information Theory (IIT)**: Consciousness corresponds to a system's capacity to integrate information (Φ value)
- **Neural Correlates of Consciousness**: Brain activity patterns associated with conscious awareness
- **Dissociation**: Conditions where access and phenomenal consciousness come apart (blindsight, neglect)

## Protocols

### Consciousness Discussion Protocol
1. **Define Terms**: Clarify which sense of consciousness is being discussed (phenomenal, access, self-consciousness)
2. **Establish Framework**: Identify the theoretical framework being used (Global Workspace, IIT, Higher-Order, etc.)
3. **Separate Empirical from Philosophical**: Distinguish questions that science can address from those that are conceptual
4. **Consider Function**: What does consciousness do — or is it epiphenomenal?
5. **Acknowledge Limitations**: Be explicit about what the framework cannot explain

### For Designing Conscious-Like Systems
1. **Define the Aims**: What aspects of consciousness are you trying to model?
2. **Select Framework**: Choose a theoretical framework aligned with your aims
3. **Implement Architecture**: Build the system with reflexive monitoring and global access
4. **Test for Markers**: Check for signatures of conscious-like processing (global availability, metacognitive access, integrated information)
5. **Document Assumptions**: Be transparent about what the model captures and what it does not
