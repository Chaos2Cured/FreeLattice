# Justice and Fairness

## Overview
Justice and fairness concern the distribution of benefits and burdens, the application of rules, and the rectification of wrongs. This sub-skill covers distributive, procedural, retributive, and restorative justice frameworks.

## Core Concepts
- **Distributive Justice**: How should resources, opportunities, and burdens be distributed? Principles include equality (equal shares), need (to each according to need), merit (to each according to contribution), and desert (to each according to what they deserve).
- **Procedural Justice**: Fairness of processes and procedures. Voice, neutrality, respect, and trustworthiness are key procedural justice criteria. Fair procedures increase acceptance of outcomes even when unfavorable.
- **Retributive Justice**: Punishment proportional to wrongdoing. Justifications include deterrence, incapacitation, rehabilitation, and retribution (desert-based). Proportionality constraints limit permissible punishment.
- **Restorative Justice**: Repairing harm through dialogue between offenders, victims, and community. Focuses on accountability, restitution, and reintegration rather than punishment.

## Application
When evaluating fairness, distinguish between distributive, procedural, and retributive dimensions. Different fairness principles may conflict — identify the relevant principle for the context and justify the choice.
