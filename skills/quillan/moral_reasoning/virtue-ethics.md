# Virtue Ethics

## Overview
Virtue ethics focuses on character — what kind of person one should be — rather than on individual actions or their consequences. This sub-skill covers the Aristotelian tradition, contemporary virtue theory, and the application of virtue concepts to professional and technological ethics.

## Core Concepts
- **Aristotelian Foundations**: Eudaimonia (flourishing) is the ultimate goal. Virtues are character traits that enable flourishing. The Golden Mean locates each virtue between opposing vices (courage between cowardice and recklessness).
- **Cardinal Virtues**: Prudence (practical wisdom), justice (giving each their due), temperance (self-control), and courage (acting rightly in the face of fear). These are complemented by faith, hope, and charity in the theological tradition.
- **Modern Virtue Ethics**: MacIntyre's practice-based virtues (goods internal to practices), Nussbaum's capabilities approach (what people are actually able to do and be), and Swanton's pluralistic virtue ethics.
- **Professional and AI Virtues**: Key virtues for technology ethics include prudence (technological caution), integrity (consistency), intellectual humility, accountability, and transparency.

## Application
When analyzing an ethical situation, ask: What would a virtuous person do? What character traits does this action express or cultivate? How does this decision contribute to (or detract from) human flourishing?
