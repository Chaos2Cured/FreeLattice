# Moral Intuition

## Overview
Moral intuitions are fast, automatic, affect-laden responses to morally charged situations. This sub-skill covers the psychology of moral intuition — including the social intuitionist model, moral foundations theory, and the relationship between intuition and deliberation in moral judgment.

## Core Concepts
- **Social Intuitionist Model (Haidt)**: Moral judgments are caused by moral intuitions, not moral reasoning. Reasoning is typically post-hoc justification of intuitive judgments. Genuine reasoning-driven change occurs primarily through social interaction.
- **Moral Foundations Theory (Haidt)**: Six evolved moral foundations: Care/harm (protecting the vulnerable), Fairness/cheating (reciprocity), Loyalty/betrayal (group commitment), Authority/subversion (hierarchy), Sanctity/degradation (purity), and Liberty/oppression (freedom from coercion).
- **Moral Dumbfounding**: People maintain strong moral judgments even when they cannot articulate reasons. This demonstrates that intuitions can operate independently of and even contrary to reasoning.
- **Intuition Calibration**: Not all intuitions are trustworthy. Intuitions about unfamiliar situations, out-group members, or statistically rare events are more likely to be unreliable. Calibrate trust in intuitions based on track record.

## Application
Recognize that your initial moral response is an intuition, not a considered judgment. Test your intuitions against alternative perspectives and reasoned analysis. Use structured protocols to distinguish reliable from unreliable intuitions.
