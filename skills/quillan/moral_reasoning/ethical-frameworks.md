# Ethical Frameworks

## Overview
Ethical frameworks are systematic approaches for determining right and wrong. This sub-skill covers the major normative ethical theories — deontology, consequentialism, virtue ethics, care ethics, and social contract theory — providing the tools for multi-perspective ethical analysis.

## Core Concepts
- **Deontology (Duty Ethics)**: Actions are right or wrong based on rules and duties, regardless of consequences. Kant's categorical imperative (universalizability, treating persons as ends), Ross's prima facie duties, and rights-based frameworks.
- **Consequentialism**: Actions are judged by their outcomes. Classical utilitarianism (maximize happiness), preference utilitarianism (satisfy preferences), prioritarianism (weight benefits to worst-off), and rule consequentialism.
- **Virtue Ethics**: Focuses on character rather than individual actions. Aristotelian virtues (courage, temperance, justice, wisdom) aim at eudaimonia (flourishing). Modern extensions include capability approaches and practice-based virtues.
- **Care Ethics**: Emphasizes relationships, responsibility, and context-sensitive response. Moral agents are embedded in networks of care — ethical decisions must attend to specific needs and relationships.

## Application
Apply at least two frameworks to any ethical question. Where they converge, confidence increases; where they conflict, articulate the precise nature of the moral tension. This is the core of rigorous ethical analysis.
