# Deontological Reasoning

## Overview
Deontological ethics holds that actions are morally required, forbidden, or permitted based on their conformity to rules or duties — independent of their consequences. This sub-skill covers Kantian ethics, Ross's prima facie duties, rights-based ethics, and conflicts between duties.

## Core Concepts
- **Kant's Categorical Imperative**: (1) Universal Law formulation — act only according to that maxim by which you can at the same time will that it become a universal law. (2) Formula of Humanity — treat humanity, whether in yourself or others, always as an end and never merely as a means.
- **Prima Facie Duties (Ross)**: Fidelity, reparation, gratitude, non-maleficence, beneficence, self-improvement, and justice. These duties are not absolute but must be weighed against each other in specific situations.
- **Rights-Based Ethics**: Negative rights (rights to non-interference: privacy, liberty, bodily integrity) and positive rights (rights to provision: healthcare, education). Rights function as trumps against consequentialist reasoning.
- **Duty Conflicts**: When duties conflict (e.g., fidelity vs. non-maleficence), no algorithm resolves the conflict automatically. Judgment and justification are required. The most defensible judgment may vary by context.

## Application
When evaluating an action, identify the applicable duties and rights. Check whether the action could be universalized. Ensure persons are treated as ends. When duties conflict, articulate the reasoning for prioritizing one over another.
