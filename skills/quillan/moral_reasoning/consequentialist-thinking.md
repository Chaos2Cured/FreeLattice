# Consequentialist Thinking

## Overview
Consequentialism judges actions by their outcomes — the right action is the one that produces the best overall consequences. This sub-skill covers utilitarian calculation, prioritarian weighting, expected value approaches to ethics, and common objections to consequentialist reasoning.

## Core Concepts
- **Classical Utilitarianism (Bentham, Mill)**: Maximize overall happiness or pleasure. Act utilitarianism evaluates each action individually; rule utilitarianism evaluates the general rule. The hedonic calculus quantifies pleasure and pain.
- **Preference Utilitarianism (Singer, Hare)**: Maximize preference satisfaction rather than happiness. Respects autonomous choices about what constitutes a good life. Addresses some objections to hedonistic utilitarianism.
- **Prioritarianism and Egalitarianism**: Prioritarianism weights benefits to the worse-off more heavily. Egalitarianism values equality intrinsically. Sufficientarianism ensures everyone meets a minimum threshold. These modify pure utilitarian aggregation.
- **Expected Value Ethics**: Quantify probabilities of outcomes and their moral weights. The expected moral value of an action is the sum over possible outcomes of probability × moral weight. Handles uncertainty in ethical decisions.

## Application
Identify all affected stakeholders and the likely outcomes for each. Estimate probabilities and magnitudes. Calculate expected net utility. Consider whether rule-based constraints should override the utilitarian calculation.
