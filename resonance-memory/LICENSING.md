## Option 1 — Open source (AGPL-3.0)

You can use, modify, and distribute this software for free under the terms of
the AGPL-3.0 (see [`LICENSE`](LICENSE)).

The key obligation to understand: **the AGPL is a strong "network copyleft"
license.** If you modify this software and let other people interact with it
over a network — for example, as part of a SaaS product, an API, or a hosted
service — you must make the **complete corresponding source code** of your
modified version available to those users under the AGPL-3.0, at no charge.

That obligation extends to the rest of the combined/derivative work, not just
the files you edited.

If you are comfortable releasing your source under the AGPL, this option is
free and requires no agreement with the copyright holder.

## Option 2 — Commercial license (paid)

If any of the following apply, you likely need a commercial license instead:

- You want to build this into a **SaaS or hosted service** and **keep your
  source code closed**.
- You want to **embed or ship** this in a **proprietary / closed-source**
  product.
- You want to modify it **without** publishing your changes.
- Your company's policy **prohibits AGPL-licensed code**.
- You want a license with a **warranty, indemnification, or support** terms
  that the AGPL does not provide.

A commercial license removes the AGPL's copyleft/source-disclosure requirements
for your product and lets you use this software in a closed-source setting.

See [`COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md`](COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md) for the template terms.

## Why dual licensing works

The copyright holder (Samuel Jackson Grim) owns 100% of the copyright in this
software and therefore has the right to license it under whatever terms they
choose. Distributing it under AGPL-3.0 to the public **does not** waive the
right to *also* sell it under a separate commercial license — the two offers
are independent, and each user picks the one that fits.

This is the same model used by projects like MySQL, Qt, GitLab, and iText.

## A note on contributions

Because the ability to sell commercial licenses depends on the copyright holder
owning (or having the rights to relicense) all of the code, any outside
contributions must be licensed under terms that permit relicensing. This project
uses a **Contributor License Agreement (CLA)** with an explicit relicensing
grant for non-trivial contributions, and the **Developer Certificate of Origin
(DCO)** for trivial ones. See [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md).
